About the Author

Tomasz Antczak

I'm Tomasz Antczak, the writer behind Stellar Lift Blog. I have a PKA black belt from my earlier martial arts training in the UK, and I currently train Muay Thai. The questions I write about come from real sessions: strength work that fits around classes, conditioning without unnecessary fatigue, sparring preparation, and recovery between demanding workouts.

I also write practical combat-sports training guides and work on Stellar Lift Training Tracker. My approach combines hands-on training experience with published research where it is relevant, particularly when discussing hydration, sweat loss, electrolytes, or recovery. I try to be clear about what a method can tell you and what it cannot.

This blog shares practical training education, not personal medical care or an individual treatment or nutrition plan. Sources appear in the articles so readers can check the underlying evidence.

What this blog is

Stellar Lift Blog is the content side of a training-focused site. Its main purpose is to publish practical, readable, and genuinely useful guidance around training, particularly Muay Thai, strength, conditioning, workout structure, exercise selection, and progression.

Hydration and recovery are also covered because they affect training quality, performance, consistency, and readiness for the next session. They support the main training focus rather than existing as separate directions for the brand.

The goal is not to publish articles simply to increase the size of the site. The goal is to build a useful training resource that helps people understand what they are doing, make better decisions, and apply the information in real sessions.

Why this blog exists

Training advice is more useful when it goes beyond isolated tips. People need context: how exercises fit into a program, how training load affects recovery, how hydration changes during hard sessions, and how to structure work across a week.

The blog provides that context while Training Tracker provides a simple way to record the actual work.

That connection is deliberate: learn something useful, apply it in training, and track what matters.

How the content is written

Style

Practical first

Articles are written to help with real training decisions rather than pad simple ideas with unnecessary complexity.

Tone

Clear and direct

The aim is straightforward explanation, useful examples, and enough detail to apply the information without making the subject harder than it needs to be.

Purpose

Connected to training

Topics are chosen because they relate directly to training, performance, consistency, recovery, or decisions people make around their sessions.
